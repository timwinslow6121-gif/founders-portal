# Medicare AEP Sales Pipeline: Planning Questionnaire

To build a highly efficient and compliant pipeline for the Medicare Annual Enrollment Period (AEP), we need to account for the frantic pace of Oct 15 – Dec 7, the strict CMS compliance rules (like the 48-hour Scope of Appointment rule and call recording), and the distinct workflows for renewals vs. new business.

Please answer the following questions as thoroughly as possible. If you aren't sure about an answer, just let me know and we can figure it out together.

## 1. Business Model & Tech Stack
*   **Team Size:** Are you a solo independent broker, or are you managing an agency with multiple agents/setters?
*   **Current Tools:** What CRM are you currently using (e.g., GoHighLevel, HubSpot, Salesforce, AgencyBloc)? 
*   **Quoting/Enrollment Platform:** Do you use a specific platform for quotes and apps (e.g., Sunfire, MedicareCENTER, Connecture)?
*   **Compliance Tools:** How are you currently handling and storing Scope of Appointment (SOA) forms and mandated call recordings?

## 2. Current Customer Retention (The Renewal Track)
*   **Volume:** Approximately how many existing Medicare clients do you have?
*   **Outreach Strategy:** When and how do you initiate contact regarding their Annual Notice of Change (ANOC)? (e.g., letters in September, mass email, phone calls?)
*   **Data Gathering:** Do you use a standardized form to have current clients update their prescriptions, preferred pharmacies, and doctors before your meeting?
*   **Meeting Format:** Are most of your renewal meetings done in person, over the phone, or via Zoom?

## 3. New Customer Acquisition (The New Lead Track)
*   **Lead Sources:** Where do your AEP leads primarily come from? (e.g., Facebook ads, direct mail, purchased leads, referrals, community events).
*   **Intake & Qualification:** Who takes the initial contact, and what are the mandatory pieces of information gathered before pitching? 
*   **SOA Workflow:** How do you handle the 48-hour SOA rule for new inbound leads vs. outbound prospects?

## 4. Pipeline Mechanics & Preferences
*   **Pipeline Structure:** Do you prefer **one unified pipeline** (using tags to differentiate New vs. Existing) or **two completely separate pipelines** (one for "AEP Renewals" and one for "AEP New Leads")?
*   **Automation Comfort Level:** How much automation do you want? 
    *   *Examples:* Automated SMS appointment reminders, automated emails requesting a list of medications, auto-generated tasks for agents to follow up if a lead stalls.
*   **Triage/Priority:** Do you need a system to prioritize leads based on intent (e.g., someone losing employer coverage vs. someone just browsing)?

## 5. Post-Enrollment & Wrap-up
*   **Post-App Process:** Once an application is submitted, what are your immediate next steps? (e.g., tracking the app status, ensuring issuance).
*   **Onboarding:** Do you send a welcome packet, a "thank you" card, or schedule a follow-up call for January (OEP) to ensure they received their ID cards?

## Answers
1.1 - solo agent that works for an agency that has 8 agents who typically set their own appointments but sometimes we have setters work for us. 
1.2 - Current CRM includes a custom designed CRM that is self hosted but have experience with HubSpot and MedicareCenter.
1.3 - MedicareCenter, HealthSherpa for Medicare, Carrier Agent Enrollment platforms such as Jarvis (UHC) and Vantage (Humana).
1.4 - Typically collect paper scopes at time of appointment (48 rule is no longer applicable as of Oct 1) but open to using MedicareCenter or Healthsherpa or any other system to record SOAs. Quo - our VoIP system automatically records all phone calls. 
2.1 - Personally I have ~500 customers, as an Agency we have around ~6000. Agents range from ~300 to ~1500 individually. 
2.2 - We send out member letters around the time ANOCs go out typically the first week of Oct. Some agents will text every customer individually, some will make personal phone calls but that is not efficient or viable for 500+ customers so we typically rely on ringless voicemail blasts and the member letters to drive inbound contact from customers. No mass email yet - only I have done that no other agent has yet but definitely something we need to add. 
2.3 - No, each agent has their own way of adding customer data, some use pen and paper, some use MedicareCenter, some use a pharmacy software called PioneerRX and an integrated software called Enlivenhealth/Amplicare, some don't save this info at all and use Medicare.gov. 
2.4 - Vast majority done in person but with the number of customers each agent currently has we are realizing that in-person can accommodate only so many customers and therefore we are doing 4 zoom meetings this year and multiple group meetings. I personally do a lot of phone calls since my office is in Charlotte and people despise the city traffic whereas the other agents are in more rural areas with much less traffic. 
3.1 - Referrals from current customers or walk-ins from partner pharmacy locations where each agent has an office inside the partner pharmacy. We have a web site and get 1 or 2 leads a month but we don't run ads or promote in any way. we will have a custom billboard in kannapolis and Salisbury but nothing in Charlotte - too expensive in Charlotte. 
3.2 - we don't have an intake process that is SOP or formalized across all agents. each agent has their own way of doing this. CMS mandates that customers must initiate contact with us first or we can call the lead if we have the appropriate PTC form (but PTC forms and compliance are not enforced at a carrier level so most agents - especially other non-agency agents - don't follow compliance with contact protocols). I typically verify the customer lives in my service area (NC & SC only) and are currently enrolled in Medicare and are interested in my services as a broker. Overall we don't really qualify leads and take appointments with anyone and wait until the inperson appt to determine if we can help or not. Not ideal but its easier to sell something in person even if its only a dental plan but that's better than nothing. 
3.3 - not an issue anymore since 48 rule is gone as of Oct 1 so we will collect SOAs in person at the start of the appointment via paper. Or we can use MedicareCenter or the carrier platform before enrollment. Paper is easiest for seniors typically but I hate storing paper copies for 10 years. 
4.1 - not sure. probably 1 unified pipeline that makes it easy to determine who is a new lead and who is a renewal. 
4.2 - depends on the agent but I want as much of it automated as possible. We use acuity and Calendly for appointments so appointment reminders are already automated which include a reminder to bring with them a list of updated meds and Drs. keeping track of what customers we have not contacted and need to, ones we haven't contacted yet but have left a VM or SMS and awaiting a reply, customers we have sent member letters to and what letter, customers that have scheduled appts, customers that have made a decision and enrolled and therefore 'done', customers that have stalled due to whatever reason - thinking about it, forgot, etc. these are the most important things for automations to help with so that we don't forget about them. 
4.3 - yes. prioritizing customers based on SAR (service area reductions), retired state employees, plans with big negative changes would be high priority. customers in plans with little changes would be low priority. 
5.1 - dont really have anything in place currently. we try to verify all submitted apps have been approved and active but we don't have a good method for verifying what happens to an app/customer once its been submitted. Typically we have to wait until we have time to verify each and every application one by one via carrier portals to see what apps stuck and which ones were cancelled by the customer or by an outside agent. 
5.2 - nothing really. a happy birthday gift card to chick-fil-a and that's about it. we don't have time to call or email or text everyone that signs up since we all have so many customers. so a 30,60,90,120 (or whatever #of days makes sense for Medicare) follow-up onboarding retention program is desperately needed for us.
