No one runs 18 back-to-back appointments for nine weeks. Notes, no-shows, callbacks and applications all come out of the same hours.

That is the whole year's growth, decided in nine weeks. An agent cannot create more hours — so the only ways to serve more customers are to stop spending appointments on people who didn't need one, and to stop losing the ones who did.

Oct 1–14 is the leverage point. We can talk to customers two weeks before AEP opens. Those 12 working days are 216 appointment slots — roughly a quarter of the season — and right now they're mostly spent finding out who needs to come in. Used to triage instead, every remaining slot goes to a customer who actually needs it.

What the portal does about it: it already knows what plan every customer is on and what changed for next year — so the "you're fine" / "we should talk" split can be sent out at scale in early October instead of discovered one call at a time.

Where the time goes today
Customers reach us by mail, text, email, and calls to several different numbers — some to personal cells, some to the pharmacy. There is no single place recording who we spoke to, what was discussed, or what was decided. After a week of 25+ conversations a day, anything not acted on immediately is unlikely to get done — and the odds fall further with every passing day. That is how follow-ups get missed and applications go in late, or not at all.
What changes
One record per customer, carrying the conversation, the decision, and the next step — so nothing depends on remembering it a week later. Agents spend October's slots on customers who need them, and go into the season knowing who those are. Time saved is capacity, and capacity is customers per agent — which is the only lever that grows the agency without hiring.
