You asked what the purpose of the portal is, and said it sounds like it's meant to keep us organized. Organization is the foundation — but the purpose is to grow Founders, by raising the ceiling on how many customers each agent can actually serve. That ceiling isn't ambition or work ethic. It's time, and it is set during AEP.

You already proved the model works

You work two days a week and they are fully booked — much of it servicing: the customers who want to sit with you because you treat them like friends. You still do plenty of this yourself. The difference is that you have help, and the help is skilled and constant. Betty, Amanda, Ben and the pharmacy staff carry the calendar, the callbacks, the applications, the birthday cards and the AEP letters.

The rest of us have a spouse, or a new pharmacy hire we train ourselves. The experienced staff are busy running the pharmacy; the ones with capacity to help are the newest, and they turn over — so most years we are training someone from scratch, during the season we can least afford it.

So we are the salesperson, the scheduler, the follow-up, the data entry and the mail room. That is the whole difference in the numbers beside this — and it's the gap the portal exists to close.

And AEP isn't the only place it pays

The off-season is servicing, and servicing is where retention is won or lost. New-to-Medicare, movers, C-SNP and Medicaid-change SEPs bring in business all year — but the bulk of off-season appointments are simple questions from customers who just want to talk to their agent. Those hours are spent, not scheduled. Answering the routine ones before they become appointments is time handed straight back to selling.

ANOC season is a data problem we solve by hand. Working out which customer is on which plan, and therefore which letter they need, currently takes days of cross-referencing. The portal already holds every policy and plan — it can produce that list in seconds, correctly, every year.

We touch a customer about four times a year, and every one is one-way. A birthday card, an AEP letter, an ANOC notice. None of them carry a way to reply, so nothing comes back and nothing is recorded. Meanwhile the same handful of questions get answered individually, hundreds of times a season.

The portal makes that a system: scheduled email and SMS through the year, targeted by what a customer actually has — their plan, their pharmacy, their doctors, their medications — so the routine answer arrives before the phone call does. That is the same mechanic as the 79% self-booking above, applied to the other ten months.

AEP 2025 — my actual call log

I ran AEP on a VOIP system that auto-texted a booking link to every missed call. No other agent had that — they used a personal cell, or the pharmacy took messages. I also have the smallest book, so the fewest calls.

51% went unanswered

310 of 617 calls — every working day for nine weeks. 40% of those people had to be missed more than once: 66 customers, 211 failed attempts, one tried 13 times before we connected. On top of that, 861 text messages with 164 people. 1,478 separate touchpoints in nine weeks, one agent, and no record of what any of them decided.

And that is only what I can see. It excludes every call and text on personal cells and everything through the pharmacy line. This is the floor, measured on the one channel that kept records — and the agents with bigger books and no system had it worse.
What already worked — and why I'm confident

Anyone who left a voicemail got an automatic text with a link to my calendar. They picked a time themselves, from my real availability, without either of us making another call.

79% self-booked

191 of 242 AEP appointments were booked by the customer, not by me — 156 kept appointments I never had to schedule. That was one crude automation on one channel.

The portal does this deliberately and for every agent: reach customers before they call, let the ones who are fine stay home, and keep the calendar for the ones who aren't.

Median booking lead time was 2 days — customers pick their own slot the same week they hear from us, without a single callback. That is the difference between a system and a spreadsheet, and it is the only lever that grows the agency without hiring.
